# Results

Every table on this page is generated by `make tables` from the JSON the
pipeline scripts wrote. None of it is typed in by hand, and `make docs-check`
fails if a table has drifted from the results it is drawn from.

Reproduce all of it with:

```bash
make data            # render the capture, about 20 minutes
make experiments     # calibrate, reconstruct, dense, pose, train, ablate, colmap
make plots tables
```

One seed, one rig, one part. The reconstruction has no stochastic component once
the data is fixed — RANSAC is seeded — so re-running gives identical numbers.
That means these figures have **no error bars at all**, rather than tight ones.
See [limitations](limitations.md#9-one-seed-one-rig-one-part).

---

## 1. Calibration

Per-camera intrinsics come from that camera's own board sweep, planned so the
board covers the whole frame including the corners where the distortion lives.
Rig extrinsics come from board stations that two or more cameras saw at the same
instant.

Two different things are reported and the difference between them is the point.
The **residual** is what you would have on real hardware. The **error against
truth** is what you would not. A calibration can have an excellent residual and
be wrong, and that is the normal failure mode rather than an exotic one.

### Agreement with OpenCV

`cv2.calibrateCamera` is run on identical detections as an independent referee.
It shares no code with `multicam.calibration`, so agreement is evidence and
disagreement is a bug in one of the two. The maximum disagreement in focal
length across the four cameras is recorded as
`headline.opencv_max_focal_disagreement_px` in `results/calibration.json`.

### Refining intrinsics inside the extrinsic solve

`make calibrate` runs the extrinsic bundle adjustment twice: once with the
intrinsics held at their per-camera values, and once with all nine freed. Both
are reported, under `extrinsics` and `extrinsics_joint_intrinsics`. Freeing them
usually lowers the residual, because more parameters always do; whether it
lowers the *error* is the question, and the record answers it rather than
asserting it.

## 2. Sparse reconstruction

Scored against the CAD model in the ground-truth frame with **no fitting**. The
bundle adjustment holds the reference station's pose fixed, so the
reconstruction frame is the rig frame at station 0 by construction, and ground
truth gives the transform into the CAD frame in closed form.

ICP is run afterwards purely as a diagnostic: how far it has to move the
reconstruction says how much of the error is a rigid offset — which is the
calibration's — rather than shape, which is the reconstruction's. Both are in
`results/sparse.json` under `cad_registration_icp`.

### What was reconstructed, and what was thrown away

The composition is in `results/sparse.json` under `composition`, labelled with
the renderer's own segmentation masks (evaluation only). The number to look at
is **background**: a turntable scan sees far more of the room than of the part,
and the multi-motion alignment plus the two-station track requirement remove all
of it.

Every rigid motion the cross-station alignment found — its size, its rotation
and whether it was classified static — is recorded under
`reconstruction.motions`. On a consecutive pair the static group is typically
comparable in size to the moving one, which is exactly why selecting the
*largest* motion is not good enough.

## 3. Dense reconstruction

Plane sweep at half resolution, bounded by the sparse cloud's own volume, with
an N-view cross check, then Open3D voxel fusion and screened Poisson meshing
with density trimming.

The dense cloud is **less accurate than the sparse points**, which is the
opposite of what a dense stage is usually expected to do. It is a consequence of
the depth sampling rather than of the matching: the sweep's resolution is set by
how finely the volume is divided, not by the image noise. More hypotheses and
full resolution narrow it, at a cost proportional to both.

Per-depth-map statistics, including how many pixels each gate rejected, are in
`results/dense.json` under `sweep`. The depth maps are also compared directly
against the renderer's own depth buffer, under `depth_accuracy`, which separates
"the matching is wrong" from "the fusion is wrong".

## 4. 6-DoF pose

Held-out stations only, selected by a fixed rule — the last 40 of the pose
session — so no seed or shuffle can leak them. Orientations are drawn from the
full rotation group, not from the single axis the turntable sweep used, which is
what separates a pose estimator from a turntable-angle estimator.

<!-- /table:pose -->

![pose](plots/pose.png)

**Read the median and the 90th percentile together.** A model-based pose
estimator on a weakly asymmetric part has a bimodal error distribution: most
frames land within a fraction of a degree, and a few land on a symmetry-related
alternative. The median describes the first group and says nothing about the
second. The recall rows are the honest summary, and failures — frames where the
estimator refused to answer — are counted in the denominator rather than dropped,
because an estimator that declines the hard cases and is perfect on the rest is
not a better estimator.

## 5. The ablation

Four experiments, all on the held-out stations.

### A. The deployment question

Classical alone against classical followed by the network. This is the question
the brief asks and the one that decides whether the network ships.

### B. The same question, controlled

Experiment A cannot separate "the network is a better refiner" from "the
classical estimate was already at the noise floor and there was nothing left to
correct". So both refiners are started from the **same** deliberately perturbed
pose, on the same correspondences, across four magnitude bands.

### C. How many iterations

The full curve from zero to four refinement steps is in
`results/ablation.json` under `iterations`, and is plotted in the middle panel
below. Refiners are usually quoted at whatever iteration count flatters them;
the whole curve is cheaper to report than an argument about it.

### D. Starved correspondences

The classical estimator is given a random subset of its matches — 100, 35, 15
and 7 per cent — which stands in for a poorly textured or partly occluded part.
If the learned refiner is worth anything, this is where it shows: the classical
stage degrades and dense render-and-compare evidence does not.

Under `starved` in `results/ablation.json`, and the right-hand panel below.

![ablation](plots/ablation.png)

### What the network is allowed to see

It is given the observed image, the rendered silhouette of the CAD model at the
current hypothesis, the rendered depth, and the observed gradient magnitude. It
is **not** given any rendering of the part's appearance. The CAD model is a
declared input to this problem, so its geometry is fair game; its appearance does
not exist, and rendering the simulator's own surface texture into the network's
input would be handing it the answer key and would not transfer to a real part.

The perturbation range it was trained over, and the measured error distribution
of the classical estimator it has to improve on, are both printed by
`make train` and stored in `results/refiner_training.json`. A refiner trained to
correct 20 mm errors and then handed 0.5 mm ones has been set up to fail; one
trained on exactly the classical error distribution has been set up to look
better than it is.

The caveats, which are also in `results/colmap.json` so they travel with the
numbers:

- COLMAP solves for every camera pose by structure from motion. Ours is handed
  the calibrated rig and solves only for the object's motion. COLMAP is doing a
  strictly harder problem.
- COLMAP's reconstruction is metric only up to a similarity. The scale factor
  the alignment needed is reported, and it is the single number that says what
  the rig calibration bought.
- Both ran on identical images.

## 8. Where the records live

Every script writes a JSON record carrying its settings, its full metrics, the
library versions and the commit it ran at. `make publish` copies them into
`results/published/`, which is committed, so the numbers on this page can be
checked without re-running anything.
