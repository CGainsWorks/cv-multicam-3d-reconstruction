# Dataset card

## What this data is

**Synthetic.** Every image in this repository is rendered by the software
rasteriser in [`src/multicam/sim`](../src/multicam/sim), from a seed, by
[`scripts/make_dataset.py`](../scripts/make_dataset.py). No camera was involved
at any point. Nothing under `data/` is committed, because a script and a seed
regenerate it exactly.

```bash
python scripts/make_dataset.py --out data/turntable      # about 20 minutes
```

## What it contains

One capture directory holds four sessions, which mirror what you would shoot
in front of a real rig:

| Session | Views | What it is for |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `intrinsics/` | 22 per camera, one camera at a time | Per-camera intrinsic calibration. The board is planned to cover each camera's whole frame, including the corners, where the distortion lives. |
| `extrinsics/` | 26 stations x 4 cameras | Rig extrinsics. The board is held in the shared working volume with every camera triggered together, in situ with the cell exactly as it is for parts. |
| `turntable/` | 18 stations x 4 cameras | Reconstruction. The part steps through a full revolution. |
| `pose/` | 140 stations x 4 cameras | 6-DoF pose evaluation. The part at arbitrary orientations drawn from the full rotation group. The **last 40** are the held-out test split. |

824 views, about 900 MB with the ground-truth depth maps, about 450 MB without
(`--no-depth`).

## Ground truth, and the firewall around it

Alongside the images the capture stores, under `truth/` and as `*_mask.png` and
`*_depth.npy` files:

- the true rig: intrinsics, distortion and extrinsics for all four cameras
- the CAD model of the part, as a PLY
- the true pose of the part at every station
- a per-pixel segmentation mask and a per-pixel depth map for every turntable
  and pose view

**None of this is read by the pipeline.** The calibration, the reconstruction,
the fusion and the pose estimator read images and nothing else. Ground truth is
read only by `multicam.evaluation`, and that separation is enforced by a test
([`tests/test_io.py`](../tests/test_io.py)) which parses every pipeline module
and fails if one so much as names a ground-truth accessor.

There are two declared exceptions, both narrow and both listed in that test:

- `multicam.pose.model` uses the **CAD mesh** — which the brief supplies as an
  input to the problem — to decide which reconstructed points lie on the part
  rather than on the turntable it stands on.
- `multicam.pose.dataset` reads the **true poses of the training stations**,
  because that is what supervised training means. The test split is selected by
  a fixed rule and is never loaded there.

The segmentation masks are used by evaluation for one specific purpose: a
turntable scan reconstructs the part *and the fixture it is bolted to*, and
there is no signal in the images that separates them. Scoring the whole cloud
against a CAD model of the part alone would blame the reconstruction for the
turntable. So the points are labelled and the accuracy is reported per class.

## The rig

Four cameras on a 76-degree arc, 0.60 m from a turntable, 0.34 m above it,
32-degree horizontal field of view, 800 x 600. Each camera has its own focal
length, principal point and radial-tangential distortion, drawn from a
manufacturing scatter; the mounting positions are perturbed from the nominal
arc, which is what the extrinsic calibration has to find. Baselines from the
reference camera are 263, 511 and 747 mm.

Sensor noise is Gaussian, sigma 0.004 of full scale, about one count in eight
bits.

## The part

A right-angle mounting bracket: 120 x 80 x 12 mm base, 120 x 12 x 70 mm
upright, a 36 mm diameter boss and two gussets. 187 mm across the diagonal of
its bounding box, 200 triangles.

It is **not strongly asymmetric**: a 180-degree rotation about any principal
axis moves its surface by 13 to 15 mm, which is 7 to 8 per cent of its
diameter. That is far above the sub-millimetre errors reported here, so ADD and
ADD-S do measure different things, but it is not a comfortable margin and both
metrics are reported everywhere for that reason. There is a test that checks it
([`tests/test_render.py`](../tests/test_render.py)).

## How this differs from real data, and what that costs

This is the part that matters when reading any number in this repository.

| | Here | A real cell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Surface appearance** | Broadband procedural texture with energy down to the pixel. | Machined aluminium is often shiny, often nearly untextured, and specular highlights move with the viewpoint. Feature density would be far lower and matching much harder. |
| **Illumination** | One directional light plus ambient, flat-shaded per triangle, constant across the sweep. | Shadows, interreflections, flicker, and a part that changes brightness as it turns. |
| **Synchronisation** | Exact. Every camera sees the same instant. | The hardest thing to get right. See [`capture/capture_sync.py`](../capture/capture_sync.py), which has been run against simulated GigE Vision cameras only. |
| **Geometry** | A procedurally generated mesh, closed and clean. | A scanned or exported CAD mesh with holes, self-intersections and a different tessellation from the part it describes. |
| **Lens model** | Exactly the five-parameter radial-tangential model the calibration fits. | Approximately that model. The residual is what a real calibration's error floor is made of. |
| **The part/background split** | The part turns, the room does not. | The same, which is why the multi-motion fit is the right approach rather than a convenience. |

The first two are the most flattering. A reconstruction that is accurate to a
third of a millimetre on this data would not be on a shiny part under
real lighting, and the number to look at when judging transfer is the feature
count per square millimetre, not the final error.

## Reproducing it

```bash
python scripts/make_dataset.py --out data/turntable --seed 11
```

The seed controls the rig's manufacturing scatter, the scene textures, the
capture plan and the sensor noise. The same seed on the same library versions
gives byte-identical images: the random stream for each view is derived from
`(session, frame, camera)` rather than drawn sequentially, so rendering a
subset gives the same images as rendering all of it.

Library versions used for the committed results are recorded in `meta.yaml`
inside the capture and in every file under `results/published/`.
